How the implied value is built

Nothing here is a hand-picked list. One function prices every row from the snapshot's own figures, and each row's detail view shows its arithmetic:

  1. Peer multiple. For the protocol's category, take every protocol whose token already trades and divide its market cap by its TVL. The multiple is the median of those ratios, so one runaway token cannot bend it. A category with fewer than 3 trading peers falls back to the median across all tokened protocols except centralised exchanges.
  2. Momentum. The 7-day TVL change, clamped to −50%…+150%, moves the value by up to ×0.35 of itself: a protocol gathering TVL this week is priced above a shrinking one.
  3. Maturity. ×0.70 for a brand-new listing rising to ×1.20 at four years tracked with no token: the longer a protocol has run at scale without issuing, the more a launch would carry.
  4. Size. ×0.55 at $5M of TVL rising on a log scale to ×1.00 at $500M: small protocols launch into thinner markets than the peers their multiple came from.

implied value = TVL × peer multiple × momentum × maturity × size, rounded to whole dollars.

The board says a protocol records no token — DefiLlama's record is not proof that none exists — and an implied value is this model's claim about a launch that may never happen, not a prediction that it will. Protocols that are wrapped, staked, bridged or versioned products of a token that already trades are left off the board: their launch already happened, under another name.
