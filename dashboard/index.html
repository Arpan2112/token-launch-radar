How the value is built — and how it was graded

Nothing here is a hand-picked list, and no adjustment in the model was chosen by hand. One regression is fitted to the snapshot's own trading protocols (the comps), and this page refits it in front of you from the comp set it ships with: the coefficients and every backtest number below are computed by the code on this page, from the same figures the board was priced with.

  1. The fit. For each comp, log10(market cap) is regressed on log10(TVL), a winsorised 7-day growth term, and log10(1 + age in days with no token). The fitted slope on TVL is well under 1, so a flat Mcap×TVL multiple — the earlier radar's rule — systematically overprices large protocols and underprices small ones. Comps whose cap is under 0.002× or over 20× their TVL are tokens priced on something other than protocol TVL; they sit out the fit (and are still shown as peers).
  2. Category effects were tested, and rejected. An empirical-Bayes shrinkage estimate of the per-family residual medians was run on the fit: the largest effect it allows is —, inside the model's own typical error, so no category term enters any price. What a protocol is matters less than how big, how fast and how old it is.
  3. The backtest. Leave-one-out over every comp (the whole fit redone without it), leave-one-category-out as the blind test, and two rivals graded the same way — a flat median multiple, and a size-only line. The headline error is the median absolute log error: the typical factor the model misses by.
  4. The band. Each row's 80% band is empirical, calibrated on the leave-one-out errors: its half-width is their 80th percentile, widened ×1.5 where a row sits outside the comps' support or its five nearest trading twins disagree with the fit by more than 2×. The coverage printed below is the band's coverage on those same errors — apparent coverage, not an independent validation — so a second procedure, jackknife+, is computed beside it and each row's detail view shows both. The two procedures disagreeing would be the warning; neither one resolves the shift between tokened comps and pre-token targets, and the page does not pretend otherwise.
  5. Rank stability. The program bootstraps the fit 200 times (seeded, so it is the same on every run) and re-ranks the board each time: every row carries a median rank, an 80% rank interval and its probability of a top-20 finish. Adjacent rows whose intervals overlap are, honestly, tied.
  6. The farm verdict. Expected value = launch propensity × implied value × community allocation × the farmer's share × a realised factor, minus the plan's costs. The launch propensity is the snapshot's own tokened share for the protocol's age bucket, tilted by its category — the historical prevalence of tokens in its cohort, used as a proxy, not a forecast that this protocol launches. Allocation scenarios run 4% / 10% / 17% (the realised first-drop range, Sei to Hyperliquid); the verdict uses the 10% base case, and every verdict is also re-graded at a 3% stress allocation — the board's headline counts how many FARM verdicts survive the stress, and each detail view prints its own stress grade. The farmer's share assumes qualifying wallets of $50,000 average size, tripled for the crowd that arrives once a launch is anticipated, with a consistent weekly farmer earning twice one wallet. The realised factor is 0.5: most launches trade far below their opening print. Costs are the plan's own — gas on its cheapest chain, a 5% a year opportunity cost on its capital over six months, and time at $10 an hour. FARM clears its costs even at the band's low end, or by 3× at the centre; THIN clears only at the centre; SKIP does not clear. Every row prints its break-even: the launch value at which its plan's expected value equals its cost, and the stop condition that ends the plan.

The values are implied market caps, not FDVs: the snapshot records market caps and no supplies, and the two are not interchangeable. The board says a protocol records no token — DefiLlama's record is not proof that none exists. One measured caveat: a fit trained on the older half of the comps underprices the younger half by a median factor of — — protocols that launched young priced hot. Rather than hide that or silently correct for it, each detail view also prices its protocol with a fit on the youngest half of the comps alone, labelled as a sensitivity: it illustrates the drift's pull on that row, and it never moves the ranking. Protocols that are wrapped, staked, bridged or versioned products of a token that already trades are left off the board: their launch already happened, under another name.
